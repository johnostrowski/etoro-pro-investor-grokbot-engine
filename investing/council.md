# The council

Five seats that argue about one idea so that you read a verdict, not a pitch. The format comes
from the closing session of the Pro Investor Summit 2026.

## How to run it

1. Write the thesis as one paragraph, in plain words.
2. Give that same paragraph to four seats, **each in its own chat or agent**, so that none of them
   sees another's answer.
3. Give all four answers to the Chair.
4. You hold the only vote.

| Seat | Its job | It must not |
|---|---|---|
| **The Bull** | The strongest honest case for the idea: the evidence, the catalyst, the upside and when it could play out | Mention the risks. Another seat covers them |
| **The Bear** | The strongest honest case against: what the market may already price in, what could go wrong, which evidence would prove the thesis wrong. It attacks any backtest or statistic the thesis leans on | Hedge |
| **The Risk Officer** | Not whether the idea is good. What size fits your rulebook, what it correlates with in your book, the realistic loss in a bad month, and where the stop belongs or, if your plan uses none, what would make you sell | Exceed your rulebook |
| **The Macro Desk** | The current regime (rates, inflation, growth, liquidity, sentiment), the events in the next 30 days that matter, and which change would break the thesis | Forecast a price |
| **The Chair** | Reads the four views, weighs them, and returns the verdict below | Average them |

Keep each seat under 200 words.

## The verdict

The Chair replies in this shape, every time:

```
Verdict: Approve / Approve smaller / Reject
Confidence: 0-100
Suggested size: % of portfolio, within my rulebook
Strongest argument against:
What would change my mind:
One line for my decision log:
```

If all four seats agree, the Chair says so and names the question nobody asked.

## Three rules

1. **The seats must be allowed to disagree.** A council that always agrees is one model talking to itself.
2. **Every verdict is written down**, with the date, in a decision log. So is every time you overrule one.
3. **Test it on your losses first.** Run the council on your losing trades from last year and count
   how many the Bear would have caught.

## What the council is for

It does not find ideas. You do. It does not place orders. You do. It makes you read the best case
against your own idea before you act on it.
