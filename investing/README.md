# The investing side

The desk that watches your book, does the research, and hands you a calm, sourced brief so that
you can decide. It never decides for you and it never trades.

It follows the build order from the closing session of the Pro Investor Summit 2026: research
first, then the council, then risk, with you as the one step that never runs alone.

## The loop

| Step | Who | What you get | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Research** | Market News, Research | A morning brief: what moved, what matters for the names you hold, at most two ideas worth a look | Before you wake |
| **2 · Council** | Bull, Bear, Risk Officer, Macro Desk, Chair | One memo per idea, with a verdict | On request |
| **3 · Risk** | Risk Officer, against your rulebook | A size inside your limits, or a no | With the verdict |
| **4 · You** | You | The decision. You place the order yourself | Never automated |
| **5 · Review** | Chief Investment Officer | A weekly page: what the book did, what changed, what the desk got wrong | Saturday |

**This engine has no execution agent.** Every seat is read-only. If you add one later, add it last,
on a demo account, inside your rulebook, and only after weeks of clean runs.

## How it connects to the marketing side

The desk's work is the raw material for what you publish. It moves as **facts, never as pitches**.

| The desk produces | The content team uses it for |
|---|---|
| The weekly performance page | The monthly report, the mid-month update, the eToro long form |
| A thesis check after a holding reports | An earnings check |
| The morning brief | Breaking + my take, clip reactions |
| The daily mood reading | A line in the monthly report. A post of its own only at an extreme |
| The dividend look-ahead | The income paragraph in a report |
| The decision log | The honest "what I did and why" in every report |

The Chief Investment Officer decides which facts reach the content team. The content team never
reads the desk's working notes directly.

## What it never does

1. Place, modify or close an order.
2. Size a position outside your rulebook, or at all if the seat is Research.
3. State a fact without a source, or fill a gap with a guess. A gap is marked *Not verified*.
4. Use a remembered list of holdings. The book is a fresh pull, every time.
5. Treat a mood indicator as a signal, or a watchlist name as a holding.
6. Give advice. This kit is educational. Automated tools can lose money quickly; your capital is at risk.
