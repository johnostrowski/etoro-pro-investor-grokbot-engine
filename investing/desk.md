# The desk — sources, clock and hand-offs

## The live book

**The only source of truth for your holdings is a fresh pull from your account**, read-only.

- Before any brief, report or answer about holdings, pull positions and the portfolio summary again.
- If the connection fails or needs re-authentication, the desk says so and stops. It does not
  estimate and it does not fall back on memory.
- Accounts you hold elsewhere are secondary. Label them separately and never mix them into your
  public percentages.
- A list of holdings pasted into a bot's profile is stale the day after. Do not keep one there.

To connect: your platform's API with a read-only key, added to your assistant as an MCP server.
Start on a demo account. Store keys where your tool asks for them, never in a chat.

## Sources

| Source | Used for | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Your account, read-only | The book, performance, dividends received | Fresh pull, every time |
| **Your inbox, read-only** | The market newsletters you already subscribe to | See below |
| Company investor-relations pages, regulatory filings | Earnings, guidance, insider transactions | Primary sources before any aggregator |
| Fund filings and the sites that track them | What long-term managers you follow are doing | Filings lag by weeks. Say so |
| Price data, a volatility index, a sentiment index | Levels and mood | Mood is context, not a signal |
| Headlines from the major outlets | What moved today | Each with a link |

### Your inbox as a source

You already pay for, or signed up to, good newsletters. Let the desk read them so you can skip them.

1. Give Market News **read-only** access to your mail.
2. List the newsletters by sender, and nothing else. The desk reads those and ignores the rest.
3. Each morning it covers what arrived since the last brief and surfaces **one to three items**
   that matter for your book, the macro picture or your own field, each with the newsletter's name
   and a link to the email.
4. If nothing is worth it, it says nothing about them.
5. It never replies, labels, archives or marks an email as read.

## The clock

Set the times in your own time zone. Each routine has one reader.

| When | Seat | What | Goes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekdays, early | Market News | **The morning brief**: levels first, then post-earnings for names you hold, then four to six stories that matter, then one to three items from your newsletters, then today's calendar. Under 300 words | You |
| Weekdays, after the brief | Chief Investment Officer | The mood reading: score, zone, one-day change | The content team. You, only at an extreme |
| Monday | Chief Investment Officer | The dividend look-ahead: this week and the next three | You |
| Every second Monday | Chief Investment Officer | A briefing on what the managers you follow have filed | You, as a short summary |
| Friday, after the close | Market News | The weekly packet: five-day prices, news and filings, analyst changes, earnings and ex-dividend dates, the week's biggest movers in your book | The Chief Investment Officer only |
| Saturday | Chief Investment Officer | **The weekly performance page**: what the book did, why, what changed in a thesis | Archived. A summary to you |
| The 1st | Chief Investment Officer | The month's expected dividends | You |
| After a holding reports | Research | **A thesis check**: intact, weakened or broken. Verdict first, then three to five sourced facts, then the bear case | The Chief Investment Officer |

Earnings news for a name you hold goes in the next morning's brief, not a late-night message.

## Hand-offs

- **Market News gathers facts.** The Chief Investment Officer and Research interpret them. Market
  News never writes buy or sell language.
- **Research reports to the Chief Investment Officer**, who decides what reaches you.
- **The Chief Investment Officer hands content the links and a short summary**, as fuel. Never a
  recap to repost.
- **Anything about your calendar, taxes or employer goes to the Chief of Staff**, not the desk.

## The watchlist

- Research is diligence, not a queue of trades. **A watchlist name is not a holding.**
- Keep the desk's watchlist separate from the book and label it "not live" everywhere.
- A new name needs a free slot under your rulebook and a case that survives the council.
- A name you removed stays removed. The desk does not mention it as a holding or a footnote
  unless you add it back.

## Ranges and targets

If you publish allocation ranges and steer by point targets, keep them apart. The desk flags drift
against the point targets. Anything public quotes the ranges. One number standing in for both is
how a stale claim gets published.
